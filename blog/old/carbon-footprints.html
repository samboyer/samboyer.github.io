Speech idea: Is it time to abandon carbon footprints?

Whether you’re working or middle class, there’s only so much you can do to reduce your footprint. Such a large percentage of it is decided by councils(?):

  • water pumping
  • police, waste collection, highways agency, hospitals, street lights etc.
  • office construction {what percentage is this compared to food/transport/heating?}

Sure, your electricity supplier can claim to only buy renewables but when push comes to shove and there’s no other sources, you bet they’ll be buying gas-powered electricity disconnecting your supply.

Q: Should we be shaming people into not flying? This is a very polarising topic, and probably deserves a post to iself. Arguments for:

  • like vegetarianism/veganism, it’s an ‘easy’ decision that can against:
  • 70% flights taken by 15% of UK population (3+ return flights); why punish rest of them?
    • what this translates to: limiting UK population to 2 return flights/yr will reduce flight emissions by 70% - much more agreeable to cutting it out entirely. Could the remaining 30% be achieved via SAF/hydrogen/electrics? food for thought.
  • ghost flights https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2022/mar/31/ghost-flights-from-uk-running-at-500-a-month-data-reveals
    • yes, it’s a horrible concept, but 500/month is 0.2% of flights; and 0.6% of flights running at <10% occupancy. Not a defence of them, but something for industry to work out, not for us to worry about.

From a long-termist perspective: we’ve only had jet planes for around 70 years, and it seems that humanity was fairly content without {them}; so even without a zero-carbon alternative, one could imagine a future without them.

(Note that we were using airships for longer/1919, which some futurists & sci-fi writers see as the future of aviation… i’m skeptical but whatever)
